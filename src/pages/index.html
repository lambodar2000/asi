About autism

A different way of experiencing the world.

Autism is a lifelong neurodevelopmental condition, usually noticed by age 3. It is a spectrum — every person is unique. Choose an area to learn more.

Read the full guide

Communication

Autism can affect verbal and non-verbal communication, language development, and the ability to express needs and feelings. Some people speak fluently; others communicate through gestures, pictures or devices.

What can help

  • Speech and communication therapy
  • Visual supports and picture schedules
  • Giving extra time to respond

Socialisation

Reading social cues, forming relationships and understanding unspoken social rules can be difficult. This is a difference in how social information is processed — not a lack of wanting to connect.

What can help

  • Clear, literal language
  • Structured play and peer groups
  • Inclusive classrooms

Sensory processing

Sensitivity to sound, touch, light or taste can be heightened or reduced. Everyday environments — a busy market, a school bell — can feel overwhelming.

What can help

  • Occupational therapy
  • Quiet spaces and noise-cancelling headphones
  • Warning before changes in surroundings

Routines & focused interests

Many autistic people prefer routine, use repetitive movements, and have intense, focused interests. These interests are often a source of joy, calm and real expertise.

What can help

  • Predictable daily schedules
  • Building learning around interests
  • Preparing in advance for change
