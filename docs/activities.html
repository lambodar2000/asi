Activities

Workshops for parents and educators, awareness drives and talks — organised by members, for members, since 2008.

Timeline of activities

  1. 4 May 2026

    Workshop on Letter of Intent & Private Trust

    Dr. Kiran Gupta guided 31 parents, self-advocates and board members through planning long-term care for a child with special needs.

    Read the workshop report →
  2. 5–6 June 2010

    Workshop on challenging behaviours

    A two-day workshop on challenging behaviours in older children with ASD, organised by ASI’s Bangalore Parents of Adolescents and Adults with Autism group with Ms. Ruby Singh of ALFAA (Assisted Living for Autistic Adults).

    More about this workshop

    Ms. Saswati Singh, Director of Inspiration Centre, Delhi & Dehradun, was the resource person. Guest speakers were Dr. Ananth N. Rao (diet, allergies, toxins and medication) and Dr. Satish Chandra Girimaji, who listed challenging behaviours often seen in older children with autism and stressed that “the parent is an expert when it comes to understanding the child”.

    Saswati Singh demonstrated holistic approaches including yoga, diet and — above all — our attitudes as caregivers.

    From participant feedback

    One of the most practical workshops I attended on autism.

    I learned that the child does not do it on purpose. They need it.

    It has given me a new direction to handle my adolescent son, with an attitude that nothing is impossible in life.

  3. 24–25 October 2010

    Vocational skills training

    A workshop on vocational training for parents and teachers of individuals with ASD, organised by ASI’s Bangalore group (Parents of Adolescents and Adults with Autism). Repeated on public demand in January.

    More about this workshop

    Facilitated by Mr. Hari Parameswaran (Dynam Designs) and Ms. Thangam Rajagopalan (head of the Dynam Vocational Skills Training Centre, Bangalore), the programme introduced the “right brain approach” to stimulate imagination — an approach that, started early, can help children grow into skilled adults.

    Every participant received a specially designed kit of tools to stimulate the senses and build dexterity. Mrs. Thangam also presented the sequence for teaching vocational skills.

  4. 2 April 2009

    World Autism Awareness Day

    In Bangalore, ASI and the International Institute for Art, Culture and Democracy held a programme at Alliance Française de Bangalore.

    More about this event

    It included a panel discussion, “Autism in India: Critical Interventions in Policy and Practice”, with government officials, autism experts and parents; readings of prose and poetry by Indian authors with autism; and a preview of a documentary on autism.

    Alongside was an exhibition of paintings, poetry and art by persons with autism, and “Art for Autism”, a collaborative painting activity for children and adults. The event received good press coverage, and “Voices for Autism” buttons were distributed.

  5. 24 January 2009

    Awareness campaign at Pedicon 2009

    ASI members distributed posters and leaflets at the paediatricians’ conference Pedicon 2009, where more than 4,000 doctors had registered. Several doctors became ASI members.

    More about this campaign

    Members created an A3 colour poster, “What is autism — signs to watch out for”, to distribute to nursery and Montessori schools along with a presentation on autism. Another group planned college visits to raise awareness about autism and the need for trained special educators and therapists.

  6. 3 January 2009

    Applied Behaviour Analysis (ABA)

    With ABA India and the Spastics Society of Karnataka, ASI screened a four-part video from the ABAI 2008 global conference, followed by questions and answers with Ms. Smita Awasthi, BCBA — at the time India’s first Board Certified Behaviour Analyst.

    The four sessions
    1. Comprehensive ABA programming — Mary Jane Weiss, PhD, BCBA: matching learner needs with instructional strategies.
    2. Developing active learner participation — Dr. Partington: capturing motivational variables in teaching and everyday activities.
    3. Because our kids are worth it — Mary Beth Walsh, PhD: a parent’s perspective on behavioural interventions at home and school.
    4. Teaching the social dance — Patricia Krantz, PhD: script-fading procedures to promote conversation.

    ABA applies principles of behaviour to improve socially significant behaviour, with a strong emphasis on making learning enjoyable and engaging learners in positive social interactions.

  7. 21 December 2008

    Understanding autism through the Rapid Prompting Method

    Soma Mukhopadhyay, author and creator of the Rapid Prompting Method — a teaching approach that has helped many non-verbal children with autism access academics — spoke to a packed hall with her son Tito Rajarshi Mukhopadhyay.

    More about this talk

    Tito, a young man with autism, is the author of Beyond the Silence, The Mind Tree, How Can I Talk If My Lips Don’t Move and The Gold of the Sunbeams, as well as many poems and essays. Mother and son answered questions from the audience.
